# BMI+

Your doctor told you your BMI is 27.4. You nodded and left not knowing what to do with that.

BMI is a ratio. It doesn't know if you're muscular or sedentary, young or old, storing fat in your belly or your hips. It doesn't tell you how many calories you actually need, or how far you are from a healthy weight, or what the actual risk looks like for your age and sex.

BMI+ gives you the context your doctor skipped.

## What it gives you (beyond the number)

- Your BMI category — but adjusted with age and sex context
- **TDEE** (daily calories to maintain your weight at your activity level)
- Estimated body fat % (Deurenberg formula)
- Healthy weight range for your exact height
- How much weight to gain or lose to reach normal range — and how many weeks at a sustainable pace
- Waist circumference risk assessment (stronger predictor than BMI for heart disease)
- Plain-English callouts: are you athletic and heavy? Older and in a different risk category? Waist putting you at high metabolic risk regardless of BMI?

## Real use cases

1. You're 6'4" and 210 lbs. BMI says "overweight." BMI+ explains that tall people are systematically over-penalized and your Ponderal Index is normal.
2. You're 55F, BMI 26. BMI+ flags that waist measurement matters more at your age and prompts you to check.
3. You want to lose weight and want to know: how many weeks at a healthy pace? BMI+ gives you a concrete number.
4. You're an athlete. BMI+ estimates your body fat and tells you the "overweight" label probably doesn't apply.

## How it works

1. Enter height + weight (metric or imperial)
2. Enter age, sex, activity level
3. Optionally add your waist measurement for better metabolic risk insight
4. Hit Calculate

Runs the Mifflin-St Jeor BMR formula, Deurenberg body fat estimate, and WHO waist risk thresholds. Everything runs in your browser — no data goes anywhere.

## Quick start

```bash
open index.html
```

No install. No server. One HTML file.
